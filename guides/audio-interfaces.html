What an interface does

An audio interface connects an XLR microphone to the computer. It supplies the 48 V phantom power a condenser microphone needs, amplifies the microphone’s small signal with a preamp, converts it to digital audio, and sends it to the computer over USB. It also has a headphone output, so the reader hears the recording while it is made.

A USB microphone has a small interface built in and needs none of this. The microphone guide covers both kinds.

What matters for narration

  • One clean microphone preamp with enough gain. A condenser microphone needs about 40 to 50 dB of gain; a low-output dynamic such as the SM7B needs about 60 dB. A preamp near the top of its range adds hiss, which raises the noise floor ACX measures.
  • 48 V phantom power. Every condenser microphone and every inline booster needs it. Every interface on this page has it.
  • A headphone output with direct monitoring. Direct monitoring sends the microphone straight to the headphones without a trip through the computer, so the voice is heard with no delay.
  • USB-C. Every interface on this page connects with USB-C and is powered by the computer; none needs a power adapter.
  • A Windows driver from the maker. A dedicated driver (ASIO) gives a lower and steadier delay than the general Windows audio driver. StellAurator connects to an interface directly through its driver, with the lowest delay the hardware allows.
  • Inputs. One voice needs one microphone input. A second input is useful for a second narrator or a guest; a second headphone output is useful for a director.

Boosters for the SM7B and other low-output microphones

A booster is a small box that sits between the microphone and the interface and adds clean gain before the preamp. It is for a low-output dynamic microphone on an interface without enough gain of its own. A 69 dB interface such as the Scarlett 2i2 does without one.

Cloud Microphones Cloudlifter CL-1

Our pick Inline booster XLR About $115 to $160

An inline booster that sits between a low-output dynamic microphone and the interface and adds up to about 25 dB of clean gain, powered by the interface’s 48 V phantom power. With it, the SM7B and the RE20 reach a good recording level without the interface’s preamp running at its noisiest setting. Manufacturer’s website

It is for dynamic and ribbon microphones only. A condenser and the SM7dB do not need it.
