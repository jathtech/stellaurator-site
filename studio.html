Inside the studio

What each part of the studio does.

Every part of StellAurator, with screenshots. The home page is the summary; this page has the details.

Casting

Give every character a voice and a face.

The cast list shows every speaker detected in your manuscript. Each character is assigned a color, a voice, and a portrait from a gallery of 445. Open Character Tags, select a character and select a portrait, or press Best fit auto assign to assign the whole cast at once. Characters without a voice are flagged, and so are lines whose speaker could not be determined, so the remaining work is visible before recording or generation starts.

  • 445 portraits across seven galleries: sci-fi crew, deep space, fantasy folk, city at night, every age, and more
  • Best fit auto assign, or assign at random
  • A voice, a color, a gender and pronouns for each character; nicknames are understood, so “Elizabeth” and “Miss Bennet” can be one person
  • Lines whose speaker is uncertain show Who says this? with the candidate names, instead of a guess
  • A warning for any character still without a voice, before any credits are spent

The cast list.

The cast list holds each character’s voice, color and portrait, with a microphone button on each card that opens their part for recording. Each character’s line count is shown, so the size of every part is known before recording.

Recording

Read a whole chapter from the on-screen script.

The teleprompter keeps the current line in a highlighted band and scrolls at the pace you set, so you can read without touching the recording controls. You can stop, reread a sentence and continue. Read as much as you want in one session, from a page to the whole book. When you stop, the recording is split into the manuscript’s lines at the pauses, on your own computer, each piece is placed on its line with its waveform and length, and those lines are marked as recorded.

Splitting by the words, so that a reread sentence is matched to its line by what was said, requires a transcription service connected under your own account. After a false start, you choose which reading to keep. A single line can also be recorded on its own, so a chapter can be read straight through and individual lines re-recorded afterwards.

  • A count-in before recording starts; adjustable pace, text size and band
  • Record one line, one character’s lines, or the whole chapter in one session
  • The sound of the key or mouse click that stops a recording is removed automatically

Hear yourself in your headphones while you record.

StellAurator connects to your microphone directly through the driver that came with it, so you hear yourself in your headphones without added processing and with the least possible delay. The headphones remain shared with your other programs, so nothing else is muted while StellAurator is open. A Reduced delay switch selects the shortest delay your hardware supports. Which microphone to buy, by budget, is covered in the microphone guide, with the interfaces and the headphones in their own guides.

  • Works with studio microphones and with ordinary USB microphones; any microphone Windows can see works too
  • Live meters for input level and background noise, a level check and a test recording
  • One gain setting for every recording made on this computer, with the unprocessed original kept on request
  • A noise print of your room, captured in a few seconds of silence and used by the noise reduction

Import recordings made elsewhere.

A chapter recorded in another program can be imported as a chapter of recordings. Drop the file in, choose the chapter, and the file is split into your lines: at the pauses, which is free and stays on your computer, or by the words, through a transcription service you connect. The file itself is never modified. Where the recording and the manuscript disagree, you decide, line by line.

  • Most common sound files: mp3, wav, flac, m4a, m4b and ogg
  • Splitting at the pauses is free and nothing leaves your computer
  • A reread sentence produces two takes on one line, and you choose which to keep

Several actors, each recording their own part.

Cast as many actors as the book needs. Each actor receives an invitation link and a free copy of StellAurator that shows only the lines assigned to them, scene by scene, with the scene direction, the character direction and the line notes above the script, and the preceding line played as a cue before each take. Their recordings are returned to you and placed on their lines, where you review, accept, or mark them for another take. The production stays on your computer throughout.

  • You choose how much of the script an actor sees: their lines only, whole scenes, or the whole book
  • Direction for the scene, the character in it, and the line, written once and shown where it applies
  • Pronunciations for the names in their lines, shown where they occur
  • Pickups are sent back to the actor who recorded the line, from the Pickups list

Direction

Direction for every line, written once.

Direction is the note a director gives an actor before a take. In StellAurator it is written once, on the line it belongs to, and shown to whoever records that line: you, an actor you invited, or a voice service that accepts direction. It is stored separately from the manuscript text and never alters an existing recording.

Actors see your direction on every take

Direction can be written for the scene (what has happened, where the characters are, the mood), for a character within the scene (what they want or hide), for a beat (where the scene turns), and for a single line. It stays on screen above the script while the take is recorded, so an actor recording remotely performs the scene as you intended without a separate briefing. Your notes are also there when you return to a chapter yourself.

Set a mood on any line

Drag the character’s feelings into place on the emotional web, with up to nine feelings at once, and set the delivery on eight faders, from urgency to warmth. The blend is named automatically (Bittersweet, Cornered fury, Grim resolve) and a direction note is generated from it, such as “Attend closely while fear and strain sharpen awareness.” Twelve presets and quick chips for narrator lines cover most lines with one click. A note you wrote yourself is never overwritten.

Auto Direct a scene or a chapter

With a text AI service connected, Auto Direct sets a mood on every line of a scene or a chapter, based on the scene so far and on who the character is at that point in the book. It uses only what comes before the line. Every line is a separate undo step, and lines you directed by hand are left unchanged. Direction is also sent to compatible voices: ElevenLabs v3 and v4 voices receive the mood as a short tag in front of the line, so generated lines follow the direction.

Clean-up

Recordings are actively analyzed for issues as you record.

Each recording is analyzed for clicks, pops, plosives, breaths, hum and background noise, and a set of fixes is suggested under it. Review plays the fixed recording before and after, with every fix marked on the waveform. Drag across a breath you want to keep and it is excluded. Apply applies the set.

A fix is a layer over the recording, not a new file. Every fix can be turned off or removed later, and the original recording is never modified. An icon on each recording shows what has been applied; click it to see the list, to show where each fix was applied, or to open the editor that applied it.

  • Clicks and pops repaired, plosives reduced, breaths lowered but not removed, background noise reduced only where it is audible
  • Background sounds (another voice, a child, a dog, a door) are detected and the line is marked for re-recording, since a sound under your voice cannot be removed
  • One fix of each kind per recording; applying it again adjusts the fix rather than adding another
  • Suggestions can be turned off per book, or limited to the issues you choose

Every take is kept.

Each line keeps every recording made of it. Generate one with a computer voice, record another, convert a third: each is kept with its waveform and length until you delete it, and you select the one to use. A spool is one complete reading of the book, made from the recording selected for every line. Spools A and B hold two complete readings side by side, and either can be exported.

  • As many recordings per line as you like
  • The newest recording is selected automatically unless you select another
  • Two versions of the book, or as many more as you add
  • Filters: computer voice, voice-changed, as recorded, not yet picked, accepted

Recordings are processed as they are saved.

Every recording is processed on import: low-frequency rumble is removed, harsh S sounds are softened, and loudness is normalized. Three tone controls adjust warmth, presence and air. Auto-level brings every recording to the same loudness without changing the performance, or can be turned off.

  • Removes rumble, softens harsh S sounds, normalizes loudness
  • Three tone sliders: warmth, presence, air
  • The click of pressing Stop is trimmed from the end
  • A pronunciation guide for names the voices get wrong

Two complete readings, side by side.

Spool A and spool B are two columns beside the lines. Select a different recording in each where you want to compare, play either spool through, and export the one you choose. More spools can be added.

Fixing by hand

Manual repair tools.

The repair screen holds the tools the suggestions are made from, available for manual use on one recording or on every selected line. The tools are grouped on the right. Selecting one opens it under the recording, with before-and-after playback, a compare button, its settings, a list of what has been applied so far, and Keep.

  • Before-and-after playback on every tool, aligned in time, with a compare button at matched loudness
  • Keep for this recording, for the selected lines, or for the whole chapter
  • Your own sequence of fixes can be saved and run as one
  • A spectrogram on which a box can be drawn around a sound to remove it

The timeline and the music

Music cues are suggested automatically.

With an AI service connected, Auto Score reads each chapter and identifies where music belongs: where a scene turns, where tension peaks, where a quiet passage needs something underneath. Each cue gets a mood and a one-sentence reason, and is tied to the words it belongs to, so it stays in place when the chapter is edited.

A musical identity can be set for the book first: your own description, ready-made descriptions, and films, series and games whose music is close to what you want. Your own music and sound effects are added under the lines from the sound library, which also imports files from your computer; music and sound effects can also be generated, through a service you connect, to match the book’s musical identity. Music can run under a scene, a short hit can sit between two lines, and a sound can start at a silence. The list of music cues can be printed for a composer.

  • A musical identity for the whole book, with how closely each chapter follows it
  • Music under a scene, swells and short hits, each with a mood and an intensity
  • Every music cue is tied to quoted words, not to a time
  • Your own music and effects on tracks under the lines, in a library sorted into sound effects, music, ambience and beds, stings and buttons
  • A printable list of music cues

Finishing and saving

Proof-listen to each chapter before delivery.

Proof listen plays a chapter as it will be exported, including pauses, and ticks off each line as it is heard. Press F on any line to mark it for re-recording without stopping playback. When every line has been heard, the chapter can be signed off. If a line is changed later, only that line needs to be heard again.

  • A tick per line as it is heard, at up to 1.5x speed
  • The book’s pause lengths, adjustable while you listen
  • Marked lines are collected in Pickups, where they can be re-recorded, regenerated, or sent to the actor
  • Sign-off per chapter

Choose the output format, and the chapter is finished automatically.

Choose the output format: Clean narration, Audiobook master for Audible’s ACX, Podcast, or your own recipe. The Finish screen then takes the chapter, or the whole book, through five steps. Diagnose analyzes every recording and reports what it found, in words and numbers. Plan lists what will be done. Apply does it, as removable layers. Check measures the result against the requirements. Export writes the files.

  • For each chapter: loudness, peaks, noise floor, and a pass or fail against ACX’s technical requirements, before and after; the check covers what can be measured, and acceptance is ACX’s decision
  • For each recording: its loudest moment, its quietest, whether its level was adjusted, and the fixes applied to it
  • Every step can be undone

Export the files Audible requires, or one chaptered file.

Export one file per chapter in the format Audible’s ACX requires, one file with chapters and cover art for other stores, or both at once. You choose which recordings are used: the selected ones, the alternates, or a whole spool. The pauses between lines are measured and corrected on export, so the pause after a sentence, a paragraph and a section break is the length you set. A muted track is left out of the export, and the export window says so before you export.

  • ACX: one file per chapter, in the format ACX requires
  • One file with chapters and cover art for other stores
  • Use only the selected recordings, or fall back to the newest
  • Warnings for anything not yet selected, not yet converted, not yet proof-listened, or muted

Generated voices, converted voices, and Audible’s rules

ACX does not accept computer-generated narration. A performance you recorded yourself and then converted into a character’s voice is still your performance, and the original recording is kept alongside every converted one. Every exported file is labeled: a file with a computer-generated voice is marked as containing AI-generated narration; a file with a reading you recorded and converted is marked as containing a speech-to-speech modified human performance. The export window reports how many lines are generated or converted before you export.

Your computers

Use it on up to three of your computers.

Sign in on a second computer and the first one asks whether to allow it to receive your books. Once allowed, the two computers recognize each other, with no code to type. A book can be sent directly to another of your computers, or kept in sync across them. While your main computer is on, its library can be opened from the other one to browse, listen and read your notes.

  • Three computers, one person, listed under Settings › Account
  • Send a book directly, over your home network or the internet
  • A removed computer loses access everywhere
  • StellAurator Nebula, the optional cloud storage add-on, carries books between your computers, encrypted before they leave: on the pricing page

Technical details

What it runs on

64-bit Windows 10 or 11 today; a Mac version and phone apps are on the way (get notified). The installer is signed, so Windows shows the publisher when you run it. The twenty-six built-in voices and every part of the studio except the optional services work without an internet connection.

What it reads and writes

Manuscripts as Word files or plain text. Recordings in mp3, wav, flac, m4a, m4b and ogg. Finished audiobooks as one file per chapter in ACX’s form, or one file with chapters and cover art. A whole book, with its cast, recordings and history, as one backup file you can open on another of your computers.
