How it works

From manuscript to finished audiobook, step by step.

Every step happens in the studio. Stop whenever you like and pick up where you left off.

  1. Bring in your manuscriptA Word file or a plain text file. The studio splits it into chapters and lines and works out who is speaking. If it is not sure about a line, it asks you instead of guessing.
  2. Cast the charactersGive each character a voice, a color and a face. Twenty-six voices come with the studio. Or read every part yourself.
  3. RecordRead from the on-screen script, one line or a whole chapter at a time, or bring in recordings you already made.
  4. Clean upEvery recording is checked the moment it lands: clicks, pops, hard P sounds, breaths, hum and background hiss. The studio suggests the fixes, you hear them right away, and nothing changes until you say so.
  5. Change the voice, if you wantRead the whole book in your own voice, then let a voice service turn each line into the character’s voice. Your timing and feeling stay exactly as you read them.
  6. Add musicThe studio finds the moments where music belongs. Add your own music and sound effects, or let it make them.
  7. FinishSay what the book is for, press one button, and the studio tidies every chapter, checks it against Audible’s rules and tells you what it found.
  8. Listen and saveHear each chapter exactly as listeners will. Mark any line to record again. Then save the files Audible asks for, or one file with chapters for other stores.

Inside the studio

What each part of the studio does.

Casting

Give every character a voice and a face.

The cast list shows every speaker the studio found in your manuscript. Each one gets a color, a voice, and a face from a gallery of 445 portraits.

Open Character Tags, click a character and click a face, or press Best fit auto assign to do the whole cast at once. Anyone without a voice is flagged, and so is any line the studio was not sure about, so you know what is left before you record a line or have one read.

  • 445 portraits across seven galleries: sci-fi crew, deep space, fantasy folk, city at night, masquerade couples, every age, and more
  • Best fit auto assign, or assign at random
  • A voice, a color, a gender and pronouns for each character; nicknames are understood, so “Okoye” and “Captain Okoye” are one person
  • A line the studio is not sure about asks Who says this? with the names it weighed, instead of guessing
  • A warning for anyone still without a voice, before you spend a single credit

Recording

Read every part yourself, at your own pace.

Your voice does not have to sound like the character. Open the on-screen script and act the scene out, every part, back to back. The line you are reading sits in a highlighted band; the rest of the chapter fades above and below it, moving at the pace you set.

Afterwards, if you want, a voice service can turn each line into the voice of the character who spoke it. Your timing, your emphasis and your pauses stay exactly as you read them.

  • A count-in before recording starts; adjustable pace, text size and band
  • Record one line, one character’s lines, or the whole chapter in one go
  • The click of the key or mouse that stops a recording is removed from it
  • Each new recording is chosen for its line automatically, unless you chose one yourself

Hear yourself clearly while you record.

The studio talks to your microphone directly, through the software that came with it, so you hear yourself in your headphones with nothing added and the least possible delay. It shares your headphones with your other programs, so nothing else goes quiet while the studio is open. A Reduced delay switch gives you the shortest delay your equipment can manage.

  • Works with studio microphones and with ordinary USB microphones
  • Live meters that show how loud you are and how much background hiss there is, a Level check and a Test recording
  • One volume setting for every recording you make on this computer; the untouched original kept if you ask
  • Any microphone Windows can see works too

Bring in the recordings you already made.

A chapter you recorded somewhere else becomes a chapter of recordings here. Drop the file in, say which chapter it is, and the studio cuts it into your lines: on the pauses, which is free and stays on your computer, or on the words, through a service you connect that can write down what it hears.

Your file is never changed. The studio only divides it. Where the recording and the manuscript disagree, you choose, line by line.

  • Most common sound files (mp3, wav, flac, m4a, m4b and ogg)
  • Cut on the pauses for free, with nothing leaving your computer

Clean-up

Every recording is checked the moment it lands.

When a recording lands, the studio looks it over for clicks, pops, hard P sounds, breaths, hum and background hiss, and suggests a set of fixes under it. Press Review and you hear the fixed recording right away, before and after, with every fix marked on the sound wave. Drag across a breath you meant to keep and it is kept. Apply does the rest.

A fix is a layer over the recording, never a new file. Every fix can be switched off or removed later, and the recording underneath stays exactly what you recorded.

  • Clicks and pops mended, hard P sounds tamed, breaths quieted but never cut, background hiss reduced only where it is loud enough to matter
  • Background sounds (another voice, a child, a dog, a door) are found and the line is marked to record again, because no fix can lift a voice out from under yours
  • One fix of each kind per recording; doing it again changes the fix instead of stacking another
  • Turn the suggestions off for a book, or choose what they look for

Keep every recording and choose between them.

Each line keeps every recording. Have a computer voice read one, record another, change the voice on a third: each stays with its sound wave and length until you delete it, and you pick the one you want.

A spool is one complete reading of the book: the recording you picked for every line, wound together. Spools A and B let you build two complete readings of the same book side by side and save either one, so you can try a different narrator without losing the first.

  • As many recordings per line as you like
  • Pick the one you want; the newest one is picked for you unless you chose
  • Two versions of the book, or as many more as you add
  • Filters: computer voice, voice-changed, as recorded, not yet picked, accepted

Your recordings are tidied as they are saved.

Every recording is tidied on its way in. Low rumble is removed, harsh S sounds are softened, and the loudness is evened out. Three sliders warm, sharpen or brighten the tone. Auto-level brings every recording to the same loudness without changing how you said it, or leaves the level alone.

  • Removes rumble, softens harsh S sounds, evens out loudness
  • Three tone sliders: warmth, presence, air
  • The click of pressing Stop is trimmed off the end
  • A pronunciation guide for names the voices get wrong

Fixing by hand

When you want to fix a recording yourself, every tool is there.

The repair screen is where the suggestions come from, open for you to use by hand on one recording or on every selected line. The tools sit on the right in groups. Choose one and it opens right under the recording, with before and after, a compare button, the settings, what has been done so far, and Keep.

  • Before and after on every tool, lined up so the same moment sits at the same place, with a compare button at the same loudness
  • Keep for this recording, for the selected lines, or for the whole chapter
  • Your own sequence of fixes, saved and run as one
  • A detailed sound picture with a box you draw around the sound you want gone

Music

The studio finds where music belongs.

Auto Score reads each chapter and finds the moments where music belongs: where a scene turns, where the tension peaks, where a quiet moment needs something underneath. For each one it writes a note with a mood and one sentence saying why, and ties it to the words it belongs to, so it stays in the right place when you edit the chapter.

Give the book a musical identity first: your own words, ready-made descriptions such as low droning dramatic or orchestral adventurous, and films, series and games whose music is close to what you want, which the studio turns into plain musical descriptions. Every piece of music is written to that identity, as loosely or as tightly as you set for each chapter.

Add your own music and sound effects under the lines: music runs under a scene, a short hit sits between two lines, and every one is a marker on the timeline. Print the list of music moments for a composer.

  • A musical identity for the whole book, followed loosely or tightly per chapter
  • Music under a scene, swells and short hits, each with a mood and a strength
  • Every music moment is tied to the words you quote, not to a time
  • Your own music and effects, as rows under the lines
  • Silence after any line, for a stretch where only the music plays
  • A printable list of music moments

Finishing and saving

Say what the book is for, and the studio finishes the chapter.

Choose what the book is for: Clean narration, Audiobook master for Audible’s ACX, Podcast, or your own recipe. The Finish screen then takes the chapter, or the whole book, through five steps. Diagnose reads every recording and tells you what it found, in words and numbers. Plan says what will be done. Apply does it, as layers you can remove. Check measures the chapter again and names the tool for anything still wrong. Export saves the files.

  • For each chapter: how loud it is, its loudest moments, its background hiss, and a pass or fail against Audible’s rules, before and after
  • For each recording: its loudest moment, its quietest, and whether its level was adjusted
  • Every step is one press to take back

Hear each chapter exactly as listeners will.

Proof listen plays one chapter exactly as it will be saved, pauses and all, and ticks off each line as you hear it. The pauses between lines are yours to set while you listen, for the whole book, and the next pause uses the new length right away.

When every line has been heard, Sign off chapter appears. Change a line afterwards and only that line needs another listen, so you always know what is left.

  • A tick per line as you hear it, up to one and a half times speed
  • The book’s pauses, set at the top while you listen
  • Sign-off per chapter

Mark a line to record again without stopping.

One button on every line, or the F key while anything is playing, marks a line to record again (narrators call this a pickup) without stopping playback. A background sound the studio found marks its line the same way.

Every marked line lands in Pickups. From there you can have the voice read them again, record them yourself, or send them to the actor who read them.

  • Mark from the line, from the review window, while proof listening, or with F mid-playback
  • The voice reads again · Record again · Send to the actor

Save the files Audible asks for, or one file with chapters.

Save one file per chapter in exactly the form Audible’s ACX asks for, or one file with chapters and cover art for other stores, or both in one go. You choose which recordings are used: the ones you picked, the alternates, or a whole version of the book.

The pauses between lines are measured and corrected on the way out, so the pause after a sentence, after a paragraph and after a section break is exactly the length you set.

  • ACX: one file per chapter, in the form ACX requires
  • One file with chapters and cover art for other stores
  • Use only the recordings you picked, or fall back to the newest
  • Warnings for anything not yet picked, not yet voice-changed, or not yet proof-listened

Generated voices, changed voices, and Audible’s rules

ACX does not accept computer-generated narration. A performance you recorded yourself and then changed into a character’s voice is still your performance, and the studio keeps your original recording alongside every changed one.

Every saved file says what it holds: a file with a computer-generated voice is marked as containing AI-generated narration; a file with a reading you recorded and changed is marked as containing a Speech to Speech modified human performance. The export window tells you how many lines are computer-read or voice-changed before you save. If a platform has given you written permission for a changed performance, attach the letter under Settings › Legal and a copy travels with the files.

Your computers

Send a book straight to another of your computers and it arrives in that library, or keep a book in step across them. While your main computer is on, open its library from the other one and browse, listen and read your notes.

Questions

Questions before you start.

Does it clean up my recordings?

Yes, as they land. Every recording is checked for clicks, pops, hard P sounds, breaths, hum and background hiss, the fixes are suggested under it, and Review plays the fixed recording right away. Finish does the same for a whole chapter, checks it against Audible’s rules, and saves it. Every fix can be undone, and the original is never changed.

Do I need ElevenLabs or another paid voice service?

No. StellAurator is a recording studio. Computer voices and voice changing are optional, and twenty-six voices come with it and work without the internet. A paid voice service is needed only for its own voices or to turn your reading into a character’s voice, and it bills you directly. Get an ElevenLabs account if you want one. StellAurator is an independent affiliate of ElevenLabs and is paid a commission if you subscribe after following this link. You can also go to elevenlabs.io directly.

What happens after the seven days?

Nothing is charged: we never asked for a card. When the trial ends, your books stay on your computer and still open and play. Recording, generating and editing wait for a plan. Subscribe when you want to carry on recording, editing and exporting.

Do I lose my projects if I cancel or stop paying?

No. Your projects, recordings and finished files are files on your own computer and nothing is ever deleted. If you have ever paid for a plan, saving your finished files stays unlocked for the work you made.

Can I cancel?

Anytime, in two clicks on your account page. Your plan stays active to the end of the current billing period.

Can I sell the audiobooks I make?

Yes. What you make is yours, and the files are the ones Audible’s ACX and the other stores ask for. You are responsible for the rights to your text and to any real person’s voice you use. Files with computer-generated narration are marked that way, and Audible’s ACX does not accept computer-generated narration.

Does StellAurator upload my manuscript?

No. It stays on your computer. Text is sent only to the voice and AI services you choose to connect, under your own account with them.

How many computers can I use it on?

Three, for one person. The studio lists them under Settings, Account, and you can remove one to make room for another. Link them and you can send a book from one to another.

Is it safe to install?

The installer is signed, so Windows shows the publisher when you run it.

Is it Windows only?

Yes: 64-bit Windows 10 or 11.
